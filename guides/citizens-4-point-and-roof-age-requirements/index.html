Citizens Property Insurance 4-point and roof-age requirements in Florida (2026)

Citizens Property Insurance Corporation, Florida’s state-created insurer, publishes its own rules for when an older home needs a 4-point inspection, how old a roof can be, and what the inspection forms must show. This guide collects those rules from Citizens’ public inspections page, sample forms, agent FAQs, and bulletins, and sets them next to Florida’s roof-age law, s. 627.7011, Fla. Stat. We link every source and note what we could not open.

Sources checked Nov 26, 2026 (America/New_York) Official sources: Citizens forms, FAQs & bulletins; Fla. Stat. No referral fees

Short version. Citizens requires a 4-point inspection for property owner, dwelling, and mobile home applications on homes more than 20 years old (Citizens Inspections page); it was 30 years until February 1, 2022 (Citizens bulletin, Dec. 3, 2021). The report must be dated within 12 months before the new-business submission (FAQ 3251). Shingle and other “soft” roofs more than 25 years old, “hard” roofs (tile, slate, clay, concrete, metal) more than 50 years old, and mobile home roofs more than 25 years old need proof of a full replacement — or documentation that the roof has at least five years of remaining useful life, which underwriters review (FAQ 2513). A 4-point or roof form is a condition report only; it does not earn wind discounts — that takes the state’s wind mitigation form, OIR-B1-1802 (RCF-1 03 25).

Inspection 1

The Citizens 4-point inspection

A 4-point inspection is a condition report on the roof, electrical, HVAC, and plumbing systems. Citizens says “the inspector must provide the age, type and condition of the wiring, heating and plumbing systems, and roof,” and that serious hazards or deficiencies “(such as exposed wiring, active leaks or evidence of leaks) should be repaired before an application for coverage is submitted” (Inspections page). The form itself says the information “only is used to determine insurability and is not a warranty or assurance of the suitability, fitness or longevity of any of the systems inspected” (Insp4pt 03 25).

What the current form (Sample Form Insp4pt 03 25) asks for

  • Electrical: main and second panel type (breaker or fuse), total amps and whether amperage is sufficient; cloth wiring, active knob and tube, or branch-circuit aluminum wiring; hazards such as double taps, exposed or unsafe wiring, scorching, improper grounding, corrosion, over-fusing; panel age, year updated, brand/model, and wiring type. “Separate documentation of any single strand aluminum wiring remediation must be provided and certified by a licensed electrician” — the form lists COPALUM crimp and AlumiConn repairs.
  • HVAC: central AC and heat, whether the systems are in good working order, date of last servicing, wood-burning stove or central gas fireplace (and whether professionally installed), space heater as primary heat, signs of air handler or condensate-line blockage or leaks, system age, and a photo of the equipment “including dated manufacturer’s plate.”
  • Plumbing: temperature/pressure relief valve on the water heater, active or prior leaks, water heater location, condition of fixtures and appliance connections, age of supply and drain piping (original, completely or partially re-piped), pipe types (copper, PEX, PVC/CPVC, galvanized, cast iron, polybutylene, ABS), and water heater age.
  • Roof: predominant and secondary covering material, roof age, remaining useful life, date of last roofing permit and update, full or partial replacement (and percent), overall condition, visible damage (cracking, cupping/curling, excessive granule loss, exposed asphalt or felt, missing/loose/cracked tabs or tiles, soft decking, hail damage), and signs of leaks in the attic or on interior ceilings. “With photos of each roof slope, this section can take the place of the Roof Inspection Form.”
  • Minimum photos: each side of the dwelling; each roof slope; water heater (including the TPR valve), under-cabinet plumbing/drains, exposed valves; the main electrical panel with its interior door label; the electrical box with the panel off; and all hazards or deficiencies.

Citizens does not require its own form. An alternate report is accepted if it is done by a verifiable Florida-licensed professional; shows the inspection date, the inspector’s signature, title, Florida license information and company; includes all the photo requirements (Citizens’ FAQ adds HVAC or other heating equipment with the dated manufacturer’s plate); and gives the age, type and condition of the wiring, heating and plumbing (FAQ 1397; Insp4pt 03 25, Special Instructions). The agent must review each form before submitting it and may not submit applications for homes with electrical, heating or plumbing systems not in good working order or with existing hazards (Insp4pt 03 25).

Inspection 2

Roof age, remaining useful life, and the Roof Inspection Form

How the roof-age rule works at Citizens

  1. Under the maximum age (25 years soft / 50 years hard): Citizens’ 2023 bulletin says the remaining-useful-life field is blocked and “no documentation is required” in that case (Mar. 31, 2023 bulletin). A 4-point may still be needed if the home is over 20 years old.
  2. Over the maximum age, but replaced: show proof of full replacement — permit or paid-in-full contractor invoice (Jan. 11, 2023 bulletin; FAQ 2513).
  3. Over the maximum age, not replaced: a 4-Point Inspection Form or Roof Inspection Form showing at least five years of remaining useful life; the application goes to underwriting “unbound” for approval (Mar. 31, 2023 bulletin). Citizens’ public page says that when a roof has less than five years left, “the property owner must provide proof of a full-roof replacement to the inspector before any policy is written” (Inspections page).

Roof age data Citizens now pulls automatically

  • From July 23, 2025, Citizens began updating missing or outdated “Year of Last Update – Roofing” data on in-force HO-3, HO-8, DP-1, DP-3, HW-2 and DW-2 policies using Verisk, “only … if it is more recent than what we have on the policy file,” with no premium-bearing change from that update (Citizens agent alert, July 23, 2025).
  • From July 2, 2026, for new-business applications effective on or after August 1, 2026, Citizens’ PolicyCenter can retrieve the year of last roof update from Virtual Roof/Verisk. If the agent accepts it, “no proof of roof update is required”; if the agent changes it, documentation is required. “All other underwriting requirements related to roof age and remaining useful life still apply” (July 1, 2026 bulletin).

What the Roof Inspection Form (Sample Form RCF-1 03 25) must show

  • For the predominant and any secondary roof: covering material, roof age, remaining useful life, date of last roofing permit, date of last update, full or partial replacement (percent), overall condition, visible damage or deterioration, and visible leaks in the attic/underside of decking or on interior ceilings.
  • Photos of each roof slope and of all hazards or deficiencies; comments describing updates (types, dates, by whom), hazards, and any roof not in good working order.
  • Signature, title, license number and type, company, and date from one of the listed licensees. The form states: “This form does not verify loss mitigation features. Use Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form OIR-B1-1802.” Like the 4-point, Citizens accepts another report if it has “at least this level of detail” (RCF-1 03 25).

Signers

Who may sign Citizens’ 4-point and roof forms

  • 4-point: “a verifiable Florida-licensed professional,” for example a general, residential, or building contractor; a building code inspector; or a home inspector. A Florida-licensed registered architect, professional engineer, or building code official whose license can be verified is also accepted. A trade-specific licensee may sign only the section for their trade — “e.g., an electrician may sign off only on the electrical section” (Insp4pt 03 25; FAQ 3825; FAQ 1394).
  • Roof Inspection Form: a general, residential, building or roofing contractor; a building code inspector; or a Florida-licensed home inspector (RCF-1 03 25).
  • Citizens may reject a form if the right professional did not do the inspection or the license “is not active or cannot be verified” (Inspections page). You can check a license on the DBPR license search; our county pages list current, active DBPR home-inspector licenses.

These are Citizens’ rules. The wind mitigation form has a separate, statutory signer list (s. 627.711) — see our comparison guide.

Florida law

The state roof-age law: s. 627.7011(5), Fla. Stat.

Separately from Citizens’ underwriting rules, Florida law sets roof-age protections for homeowners’ insurance policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2022 (s. 627.7011(5)(e)):

  • An insurer “may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew a homeowner’s policy insuring a residential structure with a roof that is less than 15 years old solely because of the age of the roof” ((5)(b)).
  • For a roof at least 15 years old, the insurer must let the homeowner get a roof inspection by an “authorized inspector,” at the homeowner’s expense, before requiring replacement, and may not refuse to issue or renew solely because of roof age if that inspection shows “5 years or more of useful life remaining” ((5)(c)).
  • Roof age is counted from the last date 100 percent of the roof surface was built or replaced under the code in effect at the time, or, for a roof replaced in parts, from the initial date of the partial replacement that eventually completed 100 percent of the surface ((5)(d)).
  • “Authorized inspector” means an insurer-approved licensed home inspector, certified building code inspector, licensed general/building/residential contractor or roofing contractor, professional engineer, professional architect, or another person or entity the insurer recognizes as qualified ((5)(a)).
  • The section does not apply to mobile home policies or to policies not considered “homeowners’ policies” ((6)(a)–(b)).

Citizens’ March 31, 2023 bulletin says its move to a five-year remaining-useful-life minimum was made “to comply with legislative changes enacted by Senate Bill 2-D” (bulletin). We have not found a public Citizens document that explains how its 25/50-year thresholds relate to the statute’s 15-year figure, so we do not interpret that here. Ask your agent how the rules apply to your policy.

Before you book

A short checklist for a Citizens application

  1. Work out the home’s age and the roof’s age (last full replacement). Home over 20 years → plan on a 4-point. Roof over 25 years (soft) or 50 years (hard) → find the roof permit or paid-in-full invoice, or plan on a roof/4-point report that states remaining useful life.
  2. Time the inspection: within 12 months of the application (10 or 90 days in the special cases in FAQ 3251).
  3. Ask the inspector to use Citizens’ current form or one with the same detail, and to include every required photo.
  4. Fix serious hazards first — exposed wiring, leaks — since agents may not submit applications with systems not in good working order (Insp4pt 03 25).
  5. If you want wind-premium discounts, ask for a wind mitigation inspection on OIR-B1-1802 (Rev. 04/26) as well; many inspectors do both on one visit. See our question-by-question guide to the 2026 form.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How old does a home have to be for Citizens to require a 4-point inspection?

More than 20 years old. Citizens requires a 4-point inspection for property owner, dwelling, and mobile home applications on homes more than 20 years old. The threshold was 30 years until policies effective February 1, 2022.

Does Citizens require a new 4-point inspection at renewal?

Not routinely. Citizens’ FAQ says 4-point inspections are required on new-business submissions, unless Underwriting decides one is needed for a renewal, case by case. They do not apply to tenant or condominium policies.

How long is a 4-point inspection good for with Citizens?

It must be dated within the 12 months before the new-business application is submitted. If lender-placed coverage was in effect within 45 days of the policy’s effective date, it must be done within 10 days before the effective date; with a prior sinkhole claim, within 90 days.

What is the maximum roof age Citizens accepts?

Without proof of full replacement, 25 years for shingle and other roofs, 50 years for tile, slate, clay, concrete or metal roofs, and 25 years for mobile home roofs. Older roofs may still be considered if documentation shows at least five years of remaining useful life; underwriters review those case by case.

Can a 4-point inspection prove my roof was replaced?

No. Since January 2023 Citizens has said that OIR-B1-1802, the 4-Point Inspection Form and the Roof Inspection Form are not acceptable proof of roof replacement. Use an approved building permit showing completed work, the county’s electronic permit record, or a licensed contractor’s proof of completed work with a paid-in-full invoice. A 4-point or roof form can document remaining useful life.

Does a 4-point or roof inspection lower my Citizens premium?

No. Those reports are used to decide insurability. Citizens’ roof form says it does not verify loss mitigation features and points to the Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, OIR-B1-1802, which is the form used for wind mitigation discounts.
