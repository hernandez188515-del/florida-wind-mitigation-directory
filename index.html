Which report do you need?

Three inspections get mixed up. They are not the same.

Wind mitigation

A survey of hurricane-resistance features — roof shape and attachment, opening protection, and related items — recorded on Florida’s Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form (OIR-B1-1802). Insurers use it for wind-premium discounts. A revised form (Rev. 04/26) must be used for inspections on or after April 1, 2026, according to the Florida Department of Financial Services.

4-point inspection

A condition report on four major systems, typically roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. Many carriers, including Citizens in some situations, ask for this on older homes. It is not a full buyer’s home inspection, and it is not the same as a wind mitigation form.

Roof certification

A roof-focused report some insurers want when a roof is older. Rules vary by carrier. Confirm with your agent what form they will accept before you book.

Where the data comes from

Home-inspector licenses were downloaded from the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation public extract on November 18, 2026 (America/New_York):

  • Profession page: Home Inspectors – Public Records
  • File: lic04home.csv
  • Field definitions: DBPR ReadMe / Disclaimer (PDF)
  • This snapshot: SHA-256 9e692086a719f4c2dbc4d24c293f5ae8aa7a4ddd06cc5eb1e9c487f857ca3678

We include only records with occupation code HI, primary status C (Current), and secondary status A (Active). Street addresses from the extract are not republished. A home-inspector license alone does not prove the person has completed the hurricane-mitigation training required to sign a wind mitigation form — confirm that with the inspector and your insurer.
